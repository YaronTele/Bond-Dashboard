How the opinion scores are built. Each theme has a list of words that suggest rising risk and a list that suggests falling risk. Every matching headline counts as one vote. The score is the balance of those votes on a scale of 1 to 5, where 3 means neutral or nothing found.

Why a score can look muted. The balance of votes is then pulled back toward neutral in proportion to how little evidence there is. Three headlines all pointing the same way land near 3.8; it takes roughly twenty to reach 4.6. Without that, two headlines containing the word “war” would produce a maximum alarm score — which is noise wearing a number, not information.

What that means. It is a blunt instrument. It cannot read tone, it cannot tell a forecast from an event, and a single dramatic word can move it. Its value is that it points you at stories worth reading, and shows its working every time.

What this page is not. It is not advice and it does not recommend anything. It gathers facts and one crude reading of them so that a person can judge for themselves.
